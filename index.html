Watching the Demo, or Ignoring It?Demonstration-Faithful In-Context Imitation for World Action Models

Anonymous Authors

Abstract

In-context imitation lets a robot policy perform an unseen task from a single demonstration without finetuning. World action models (WAMs) acquire this ability by predicting the future video and decoding actions from it. Yet such policies are evaluated by task success, which hides whether the demonstration is followed: the strongest of them selects the demonstrated object but often leaves its route. The reason is that existing in-context WAMs decode the action from imagined frames without attending to the demonstration, which thus shapes what the robot imagines more than what it does. We present RoboKata, an in-context WAM whose actions stay faithful to the demonstration. Demo Cross-Attention lets the action branch read frozen DINOv2 tokens of the demonstration through a gated cross-attention. Foresight Indexing predicts the future in the same feature space and uses this prediction to index the demonstration frames that the next action reads, without alignment labels. RoboKata is trained on counterfactual pairs that change the demonstration along one axis at a time. To measure demonstration use, we build RoboKata-Bench, which fixes the query scene, changes the demonstration along five axes, and counts a trial as a strict success only if the robot passes the demonstrated waypoints. On RoboKata-Bench, RoboKata matches the strongest baseline in task success and raises the strict success rate from 51.2% to 63.2%. With human-video prompts, it also raises the zero-shot success rate on six unseen RoboTwin 2.0 tasks from 45.0% to 54.3%.
